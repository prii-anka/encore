Encore

Every road trip writes its own playlist. Encore makes sure you get to keep it.

A music-app feature that notices when a group listening session ends and turns every song you played into a saved, shareable playlist in one tap.

The problem

The best playlists get built live, one shouted request at a time. When the trip ends, the app forgets all of it.

The bet

If we offer to save a session at the right moment, people will keep it and press play on it again next trip.

The solution

Zero effort during the trip, one quiet card after it, and a playlist that comes back the next time you get in the car.

Success looks like

25% of shown cards saved, half of those replayed within 30 days. Targets for a pilot, not results.

Why it matters beyond engagement

Music is one of the strongest triggers we have for personal memory. In a UC Davis study, students recognized about 17 of 30 song clips, and roughly 13 of those were tied to a specific memory (Janata, 2009). A trip playlist is not just a list. It is a souvenir, which shaped how Encore names, dresses and returns your sessions.

03 · Research

Users had been asking for this for years.

I started with what people already say in public, then audited what exists, then planned interviews to test what I could not learn from forums.

What people are saying

Paraphrased from Spotify Community feature requests and threads.

A dad and daughter jammed all night, then wanted every song they played as a playlist the next morning.
Two hours of trading songs on a road trip, and they wanted the playlist as a memory of it.
Autoplayed songs in a Jam never show up in listening history, so the best surprises are the first to vanish.
One user built their own "Jam Saver" web tool. When people build workarounds, the need is real.

04 · Insights

The problem is not saving. It is timing.

1
Nobody curates while vibing

Singing, driving, talking. Tapping "add to playlist" 36 times is not happening.

2
Value shows up afterwards

You realize a session was special right when the app forgets it.

3
It is a memory, not a list

People describe these playlists as souvenirs. Naming and cover matter.

4
The next trip starts cold

The real payoff is not saving. It is pressing play next time without asking.

How might we
help people keep the soundtrack of a moment, without asking them to do anything while it is happening?
05 · Exploration

Three directions, judged on effort in the moment.

The obvious answer was a "Save queue" button. I explored it alongside two alternatives and scored all three against the insights, not my taste.

Direction A
Save queue button

A button in the queue view. Simple, but it only works if someone remembers to tap it mid-trip.

Rejected: fails insight 1
Direction B
Sessions history tab

Every session logged in Library. Powerful, but it is a place you must think to visit.

Kept as the fallback surface
Direction C
Encore: ask once, afterwards

The app spots the end of the session and offers the save on Home.

Chosen
CriteriaA · ButtonB · HistoryC · Encore

06 · The hard part

Knowing when a session is over.

The screens were the easy part. One false prompt after a boring commute and people turn the feature off forever, so I worked out the session model before any screens.

1 · Signals
Something ends
Car audio disconnects, a 30+ minute pause, or the phone leaves a speaker
2 · Session engine
Clean the list
Keep play order, drop skips under 10 seconds, remove duplicates, include Jam and autoplay tracks
3 · Worth asking?
Quality gate
10+ songs, not mostly one existing playlist, not a private session
4 · Encore card
Ask once, quietly
A card on Home next app open. Waits 7 days, then expires into session history

Sessions that fail the gate still land in the history tab from Direction B, so nothing is ever truly lost. The gate only decides whether we interrupt you.

11 · Reflection

The smallest detail did the most work.

What surprised me

I expected the save button to be the hero. Walking through the flow, the auto-generated name carried more of the feeling than anything else. "Sedona Road Trip" turns a list into a memory, so naming moved into the first release instead of a later one.

What I would do differently

Bring data science in on day one. I sized the opportunity from public numbers; real session logs would have told me the true share of 10+ song car sessions in a week.

What is next

Run the ride-along interviews, prototype the card in a real car, and test two copy variants before a single line of production code.
